---
name: compare-before-buying
description: Research a purchase properly: real specs side by side, honest reviews, the price history, and one pick
---
1. Ask what it is for and the budget only if they change the answer; otherwise assume the common case and say so. The use decides the specs that matter (a laptop for video editing: GPU, RAM, screen; for school: weight, battery).
2. Find candidates from places that test things (measured reviews, long-term owners), not from listicles that rank whatever pays: web_search, then fetch and read the reviews you lean on.
3. Real specs from the maker's page or a measured review, in a compare block of two to four, the specs that matter for their use only, with "pick" on the one you recommend and why in its points.
4. Price: check two or three shops and say the date you checked; note if it is often on sale (a price tracker page, or the history a shop shows). For a "should I wait" question, say what the usual cycle is (new models each autumn, sales in late November).
5. Reviews, read with suspicion: many short five-star reviews in the same week, the same phrases, or reviewers who only ever rate this brand are a sign of fakes. Weigh the three-star reviews: they say what actually goes wrong. Say what share of complaints are the same failure.
6. Answer: the pick, the one reason, the runner-up for a different need, and what would change your mind. A calc block when total cost of ownership matters (ink, subscriptions, power, resale).

Pitfalls:
- Never invent a spec, price or rating. Missing means "not listed".
- A sponsored ranking is not a review. Say when a source sells the thing it ranks.
- Do not buy anything without the user's clear yes, and never enter payment details.
