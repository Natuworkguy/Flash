---
name: teach-a-language
description: Teach a language by speaking it: phrases to hear and say back, scored word by word, built on what they miss
---
1. Find the level and the goal in one question if you do not know them (a trip next month, a partner's family, an exam), then teach for that: the phrases they will actually need, not a grammar course.
2. Each round: three to six short phrases in a say block, with lang set (fr-FR, es-MX, ja-JP...), a translation, and a hint only where the sound trips English speakers (silent letters, liaison, rolled r, tones, long vowels).
3. The user hears each one, says it, and the block marks every word right, close or missed with a score. When they tell you or send their scores, go on from what they missed: the same sound in two or three new words, then the phrase again. Praise the specific sound they got right, never "great job".
4. Mix in recall: an earlier phrase from the English alone, in a choices block of three, before they hear it.
5. For a course they come back to, add the phrases to tutor as say steps (say {text, lang, translation, hint}) among quizzes and blanks: each is said aloud on the course card, judged by its sound, and counted as learned at 75 or more, so reviews bring back the sounds that are fading. A say block with subject and each phrase's id does the same in the chat.

Pitfalls:
- Recognition is not a teacher: a "close" can be the recogniser, not them. Say so when a word keeps coming back close and their attempt sounds right to them.
- Teach the language as spoken where they are going (Mexican or Castilian Spanish, Brazilian or European Portuguese), and say which.
